Key Innovation

AVR benefit in octogenarians is symptom-independent — unlike non-octogenarians where the benefit depends on symptoms (interaction p=0.04 vs p=0.6).


Clinical Implication

  • Symptomatic octogenarians: HR 0.28 (p<0.01)
  • Asymptomatic octogenarians: HR 0.29 (p<0.01)
  • Asymptomatic non-octogenarians: HR 0.60 (p=0.10, NS)

Both symptomatic and asymptomatic octogenarians benefit equally from AVR.

Sensitivity Analysis: E-Value Robustness

How strong would an unmeasured confounder need to be to nullify the AVR benefit?

E-value = HR−1 + √(HR−1 · (HR−1 − 1))
FindingHRE-Value
(point)		E-Value
(CI)
AVR mort. (Oct)0.286.609.39
AVR mort. (Non-Oct)0.384.726.82
AVR mort. (Oct sympt)0.286.609.81
AVR mort. (Oct asympt)0.296.3511.88
AVR hosp. (Oct)0.622.683.55

What E-Value = 6.60 Means

An unmeasured confounder would need to be associated with both AVR treatment and mortality by a risk ratio of at least 6.60 to explain away the observed HR of 0.28.


For Context

  • Smoking → lung cancer: RR ≈ 10–20
  • Obesity → diabetes: RR ≈ 3–7
  • Required confounder: RR ≥ 6.60 for both exposure and outcome

Conclusion: It is implausible that unmeasured confounding alone explains the AVR mortality benefit. The finding is robust.
